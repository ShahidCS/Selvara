# Phase 4 independent audit: AUD3, privacy and governance (items 7, 8, 16, 19, 20, 21)

Auditor: independent external reviewer (read-only). Finding prefix: P4-A3-. Date: 2026-11-23.

## 1. Summary

I audited the back-end v2 design in `docs/architecture/backend-v2/` against checklist items 7, 8, 16, 19, 20 and 21. The main evidence was 2B-classification, 2B-data-model §7, 2G (G-0 to G-13), 2D D-12, 2A A-LC-08 and the NFRs, 2H H-9 and H-11, 2I I-4 and I-5, 2J J-SHR, 2K, ADR-001 to ADR-014, the Phase 2 and Phase 3 summaries, and the four registers. Owner decisions OQ-16 to OQ-25 were checked against the workstream texts.

The privacy design is unusually thorough. It has a field-level classification with E0/E1/E2 classes, a retention class per table and a deletion mode per table. Crypto-shredding is designed at three levels (tenant, subject and file) with a stated 8-day backup window. It also has a deletion-certificate schema, a full Annex A table and thirteen ADRs, each with real alternatives.

Several requirements still fall short when tested strictly:
- The Supabase `auth` schema holds personal data in plaintext, and no document classifies it.
- The customer-readable final export at offboarding is described in 2G but not designed in 2D, 2C or 2J, and 2D points the offboarding flow at the DEK-encrypted backup export.
- The ISO 27001/27701 gaps are listed, but none has a remediation plan.
- The owner's uniform-SLA decision (OQ-23) is neither costed nor reflected in the NFRs.
- No plan price book or tenant projection exists to test viability against.
- MVP feasibility with two approvers, no engineers, no timeline and no budget is asserted but not shown.
- Several owner decisions (OQ-21 trial values, OQ-25 billing retention, OQ-23 SLA) were not carried into the workstream texts that implement them.

**Overall opinion: qualified.** Items 7, 8, 16, 19 and 20 are Partial. Item 21 fails. No finding is Critical, but P4-A3-08 becomes Critical if D-12 is built as written.

## 4. Positive observations

- The classification covers every tenant entity in M01 to M26 and every control-plane table except `auth.*`. Each field has a class, an encryption class (E0, E1 or E2) and a retention class. Each table has a deletion mode and a retention anchor. Column-level purges (B-RC30 to B-RC35) remove high-risk fields earlier than their rows.
- Crypto-shredding is designed at tenant, subject and file granularity. Wrapped keys live only in `ks` (DA-04), and the window for erasure to reach backups is stated and bounded (8 days, B-K05, DA-11) and disclosed in the DPA (G-DPA-12). E0 plaintext residue in PITR is disclosed honestly.
- The deletion-certificate schema (G-6.3) is specific: per-store counts before and after, KMS log event ids including multi-Region replicas, and a signature anchored in the platform audit log and the external WORM anchor.
- Every proposed ADR (ADR-002 to ADR-014) has real alternatives with pros and cons, a security and privacy section, and verification tests.
- The Annex A mapping covers all 93 controls with honest O and P ratings for organisational controls. The 27701 controller and processor split matches the two capacities set out in G-0 and G-2.
- The cost model was corrected in Phase 3 (DA-13), and every per-tenant cost driver is an enforceable entitlement key (I-4.5.2).
- 2K is candid about delivery risk: it commits no calendar, sets gated releases, and makes parity a release criterion.
