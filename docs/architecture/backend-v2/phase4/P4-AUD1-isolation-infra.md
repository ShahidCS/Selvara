# Phase 4 audit AUD1: tenant isolation and infrastructure (checklist items 1 to 5, 11, 14, 15)

Finding ID prefix: P4-A1-. Auditor stance: independent, read-only. All compliance positions are draft pending qualified review.

## 1. Summary

I audited the back-end v2 design in `docs/architecture/backend-v2/` against checklist items 1 to 5, 11, 14 and 15. My main sources were Spine v2 (S1 to S8), ADR-001 to ADR-005, ADR-009, ADR-010 and ADR-013, and 2A, 2F (F-4, F-7, F-8, F-11, F-12), 2G G-8 and 2H (H-1 to H-9). I also checked the registers (risks, decisions, assumptions, open questions with OQ-16 to OQ-25) and the Phase 3 reports, including P3-X3's VC list. I judged the design against ADR-001 as it was accepted. I did not reopen the schema-per-tenant choice, but I rated each item honestly.

The isolation design inside the database is unusually concrete and testable. It has one router, transaction-scoped roles, forced RLS, `route_mac`, a fail-closed routing path and an escape test suite that documents R-56 honestly. The control plane is well bounded: a separate `cp` project, an allowed catalogue enforced in CI, narrow function grants and a stated blast radius. Backups and keys are designed in depth.

I found weaknesses in four places:
- **Key-store continuity.** In a Supabase organisation compromise, or in a region loss without a cross-region replica, the design permanently loses every file and every E2 field older than 8 days. The tenant exports do not escrow subject or file keys.
- **Unverified vendor facts.** Several RPO/RTO claims depend on vendor capabilities that are still unverified (VC-02, VC-14, VC-16, DU-02). Where those facts fail, the fallbacks turn the outcome into data loss.
- **Logs and secrets.** Postgres, pgaudit and rotation logging are not specified tightly enough to keep secrets and E0 tenant values out of shared logs.
- **Residency.** Residency is a placement convention, not a per-tenant technical control.

Item 2 is **Partial by design**, as ADR-001 predicted. The owner's acceptance (R-02, OQ-16) supports that Partial; it does not raise it to Pass.

## 4. Positive observations

- R-56 is stated honestly. The design does not claim the attack fails; it lists prevention and detection controls and the tests that prove them (DA-02, F-ISO-06, V-002-01).
- Fail-closed routing is concrete: uncached session, membership and status reads, an HMAC on routing rows (`route_mac`), a validated `tkey` pattern, no default `search_path`, and an assertion after the preamble.
- The control-plane catalogue is enforced in CI (V-010-01/02), `app` and `console` are separate deployments (R2-03), and the blast-radius table (F-8.2) is explicit.
- Single-tenant restore path A is per-schema, with four-eyes approval, verification and an atomic swap, so other tenants are not affected. Off-site copies sit in a separate account and region under compliance lock.
- The crypto-shredding window is bounded and evidenced (an 8-day `ks` roll-off with tombstones in the journal), and KEK replicas are included in destruction.
- The migration orchestrator has a canary, batches, health gates, a version window, a per-tenant state machine, and drift that fails closed on security-relevant objects.
- The secrets inventory (F-11) names, for every secret, where it is stored, its reference, rotation and owner. There are no secrets in Vercel env vars, and there is a fallback for VC-10.
